# Investment committee memo: UK charity multi-asset fund shortlist

**Client:** Northbridge Education Charity, a fictional case  
**Evidence date:** 21 September 2026  
**Decision:** determine which managers should advance to the next due-diligence stage  
**Scope:** public-information review of three exact GBP accumulation share classes  
**Not covered:** manager interviews, operational due diligence, legal or tax advice, or a recommendation to transact

## Recommendation

Do not appoint any of the three funds from public documents alone.

Advance Cazenove first as the balanced candidate for manager discussion. Retain M&G as the cost-led comparator and Sarasin as the mission-led alternative. The base score is Cazenove 70, with M&G and Sarasin both at 63, but the leader changes when trustee priorities change. Cazenove leads the base and preservation-first cases, M&G leads when fees receive an intentionally extreme 45% weight, and Sarasin leads when mission protections dominate.

This result does not justify an immediate two-manager solution. The reviewed funds remain substantially equity-led and the available evidence does not establish complementary risk sources. Splitting the mandate would add monitoring and implementation demands without yet demonstrating a portfolio benefit.

The committee should approve a **conditional shortlist**, not a final manager selection. A final recommendation should return to the committee only after comparable downside data, full cost reconciliation, stressed-liquidity evidence and product-level mission exposure have been obtained.

## Client mandate

The fictional charity has a £10 million long-term investment pool and a separate £450,000 reserve, equal to 18 months of planned spending. Annual spending is £300,000, or 3% of the long-term pool. The investment horizon exceeds ten years.

The discussion objective is UK CPI + 3% a year after fund costs over rolling five-year periods. If achieved, that return would approximately fund 3% spending while maintaining the real value of the capital before other cash flows. It is not a forecast. A fall near 15% triggers trustee review, but it is neither a guaranteed loss limit nor an automatic instruction to sell.

Daily or weekly dealing is required. The charity also requires explicit look-through assessment of tobacco, controversial weapons and investments that could conflict with its education mission. Charity Commission guidance supports a documented investment policy, appropriate manager selection and continuing monitoring [G1].

The mandate creates four competing questions:

1. Can the strategy plausibly support spending and real capital preservation after costs?
2. Can trustees understand and tolerate the sources of loss?
3. Are liquidity and implementation terms workable in normal and stressed markets?
4. Are mission protections clear enough for the trustees to defend?

No single historical return number can answer all four.

## Suitability screen

The review covers:

- SUTL Cazenove Charity Multi-Asset Fund, Class Z Accumulation GBP, ISIN GB00BF783Y68
- Sarasin Endowments Fund, A Accumulation GBP, ISIN GB00BYZJN999
- M&G Charity Multi Asset Fund, Sterling Class Accumulation, ISIN GB00BK1KFR05

All three pass the basic eligibility, exact-share-class, dealing and published-history screens. Cazenove and Sarasin state direct inflation-plus objectives. M&G instead targets a 75% equity / 25% bond comparator and at least 3% annual yield, so its fit with the charity's CPI + 3% objective requires further interpretation [C1–C3; S1–S2; M1–M2].

Mission evidence is not equally complete. Sarasin provides the clearest product-level exclusions, although indirect holdings may not apply every screen. The reviewed Cazenove and M&G documents do not establish comprehensive binding product-level tobacco and controversial-weapons exclusions across the full portfolio [C2–C5; S2, S4–S5; M4].

Most importantly, none of the three has sufficient public evidence for final appointment. Maximum drawdown, recovery time, comparable monthly returns, complete look-through costs, stressed liquidity and several governance questions remain missing or inconsistent.

## Comparative evidence

### Cazenove: balanced diligence starting point

Cazenove has the strongest base score because its real-return objective, cost level, diversified process and charity-specific governance provide the best balance across the six dimensions. Its current objective is to exceed UK CPI + 4% after fees over rolling ten-year periods. The latest disclosed ten-year return was 6.6% a year against the manager's 7.9% disclosed target series, so attribution is required. That historical target series is not homogeneous: the factsheet uses RPI to 30 June 2018 and CPI thereafter, and the pre-conversion period largely reflects the predecessor vehicle [C1].

The fund reported 76.7% equities, with bonds, property, diversifiers and cash making up the remainder. That is a broad asset mix, but equities remain the main risk source. The 0.65% estimated OCF is below Sarasin's, while the disclosed 0.13% transaction cost uses a different date and definition and should not be treated as a verified all-in total [C1, C4].

Charity-specific reporting and a CAIF Advisory Committee strengthen governance. The main unresolved issues are the absence of a verified maximum-drawdown and recovery series, incomplete look-through cost reconciliation, potential affiliated-manager conflicts and weak product-level mission evidence [C3–C5].

### M&G: cost-led comparator

M&G has the lowest disclosed cost. The official illustration reports a 0.60% total product cost, while the OCF is 0.50% and the disclosed transaction cost is 0.098% [M1–M3]. It also reported a positive 0.8% return in calendar 2022. That observation is useful, but it does not establish lower downside risk because the stress periods are not aligned and a maximum-drawdown series is unavailable.

The fund can hold 60% to 90% in equities and uses 15 underlying holdings, many affiliated with M&G. The five-year net return was 8.16% against an 8.68% 75/25 comparator [M1–M2]. This was the smallest disclosed target gap among the three, but the comparator and yield objective are not equivalent to CPI + 3%.

Before M&G could be preferred, the manager should explain look-through duration and equity risk, underlying fee rebates, affiliated-fund selection, full mission rules and how the yield target affects total return. Its low cost is attractive only if objective and governance fit are also adequate.

### Sarasin: mission-led alternative

Sarasin has the strongest public evidence on exclusions and stewardship. Its published policies address tobacco, weapons, gambling, alcohol and adult entertainment, although the application to indirect external funds still requires look-through confirmation [S2, S4–S5].

The strategy states a UK CPI + 4% after-fees objective over rolling five-year periods. The latest five-year net return was 4.85% against a 9.26% target, and calendar 2022 performance was -9.8% [S1–S3]. The disclosed OCF is approximately 0.93% to 0.94%, with a 0.16% transaction cost, making it the highest-cost candidate before any omitted underlying closed-ended-fund costs [S1, S6].

The main issue is not merely that returns were weaker. Public materials show both 0.93% and 0.94% OCF figures, so the current charge should be reconciled before reliance [S1, S6]. Sarasin remains relevant when mission protection is the dominant trustee priority, but the committee should not pay a higher fee without a satisfactory explanation of performance, costs and document governance.

## Scorecard and sensitivity

The base score uses the following weights:

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Client objective fit | 25% |
| Risk and downside evidence | 25% |
| Diversification and process | 15% |
| Fees and implementation | 15% |
| Mission and stewardship | 15% |
| Governance and reporting | 5% |

Each dimension is scored from 1 to 5, using a written rationale. The weighted total is scaled to 100. The result records the strength of current evidence against the fictional mandate; it does not forecast future performance.

| Trustee priority | Cazenove | Sarasin | M&G | Numerical leader |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Base | 70 | 63 | 63 | Cazenove |
| Preservation-first | 70 | 64 | 62 | Cazenove |
| Cost-first | 76 | 55 | 77 | M&G |
| Mission-first | 65 | 67 | 58 | Sarasin |

The base and preservation-first cases favour Cazenove more clearly, while the deliberately extreme cost-first case gives M&G only a one-point lead and the mission-first case gives Sarasin a two-point lead. Those changes show that the decision still depends on trustee priorities. The cost-first case is a stress test, not a proposed default allocation policy. The ranking shows which questions to prioritise, not which contract to sign.

## Conditions before appointment

The following evidence is required before a final recommendation:

1. **Comparable downside history.** Obtain monthly net returns for the current legal vehicles, maximum drawdown, recovery time, downside attribution and agreed stress tests.
2. **Performance attribution.** Explain each objective miss and separate strategic asset allocation, manager selection, tactical decisions and implementation costs.
3. **Full cost reconciliation.** Confirm OCF, transaction costs, underlying-fund charges, fee rebates, spreads and any dilution levy for a £10 million mandate.
4. **Mission look-through.** Confirm binding product-level rules and current exposure to tobacco, controversial weapons and other restricted activities across direct and indirect holdings.
5. **Stressed liquidity.** Provide look-through liquidity buckets, valuation frequency, redemption terms, suspension history and a stressed £10 million redemption analysis.
6. **Governance and conflicts.** Document decision rights, team succession, advisory-committee independence, affiliated-fund selection and conflict oversight.

Operational due diligence should separately cover legal structure, custody, valuation controls, dealing, business continuity, cyber controls and error management. These questions cannot be answered by the investment scorecard.

## Monitoring after any appointment

Trustees should:

- review objective performance and attribution at least annually;
- convene a review near a 15% drawdown without imposing an automatic sale;
- assess any lead-manager departure, material team turnover or change in decision rights;
- review material changes in equity, alternatives, duration, currency or affiliated-fund exposure;
- escalate any mission-screen breach or weakening of exclusions;
- review any OCF increase, new performance fee or unreconciled underlying cost;
- review any redemption deferral, suspension or deterioration in look-through liquidity; and
- escalate unresolved disclosure discrepancies or delayed risk and holdings reporting.

## Limitations

This is a hypothetical educational case using a narrow, manually selected three-fund universe. It may contain selection and survivorship bias. Official fund documents are primary sources but are generally produced by the managers and are not independent verification. Data dates, objectives and cost definitions differ. Some performance histories incorporate predecessor vehicles.

The project does not calculate a common-period return series, volatility, maximum drawdown, recovery time, beta, correlation or manager alpha. It does not conduct manager interviews, operational review, legal review, custody analysis, tax analysis or independent holdings verification. Whole-number scores and scenario weights require judgement; sensitivity analysis makes that judgement visible but does not remove it.

## Committee action requested

Approve Cazenove, M&G and Sarasin for conditional next-stage review in the roles described above. Do not approve an investment or a two-manager allocation at this stage. Require the six evidence packages before a final recommendation returns to the committee.
